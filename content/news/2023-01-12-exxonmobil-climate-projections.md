An [article](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abk0063) published in *Science* today demonstrates that ExxonMobil scientists have been accurately predicting global warming since the 1980s, even as the company publicly downplayed the link between carbon dioxide emissions and global warming. To quantitatively evaluate the climate projections published in internal company memos, the team of researchers led by Dr. Geoffrey Supran used the *implied Transient Climate Response (iTCR)* method we first [published](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019GL085378) in *Geophysical Research Letters* in 2020 (and which features prominently in the [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)'s Sixth Assessment Report](https://www.ipcc.ch/assessment-report/ar6/)).

The authors compared ExxonMobil's projections with the 69% average skill score that Hausfather, Drake, Abbott, and Schmidt (2020) calculated for 18 academic and government climate model projections published between 1970 and 2007, concluding that ExxonMobil scientists' global warming projections were as skillful as those of independent scientists of their day — and that the company's own models were especially skillful. ([Supran et al. 2023, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abk0063))

These new results are likely to be cited in ongoing [lawsuits](https://perma.cc/ZHZ3-5HTX) by cities, counties, and states accusing ExxonMobil and others of deceptive marketing, misleading shareholders, and culpability for climate change.

While this new research is quite far from the focus of our research group, it is a perfect illustration of how even the most basic of climate science research (including ours!) can advance the global movement for climate justice (see also the [recent essay](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-022-03347-6) Geoffrey Henderson and I published in *Climatic Change*).
